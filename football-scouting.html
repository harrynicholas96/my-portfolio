Designing a Professional Football Scouting Platform That Drives Strategic Recruitment Decisions

Led end-to-end product strategy and UX for a mobile-first platform that transformed how professional football clubs evaluate players, moving from fragmented tools to a unified system that improved decision-making velocity and consistency across scouting operations.

Lead Product Designer Discovery to MVP Launch Sports Technology Mobile-first UX

Discovery & Strategic Framing

Professional football scouting relies on rapid, high-stakes decision-making—yet scouts were operating across disconnected tools with inconsistent evaluation frameworks. The business challenge wasn't just improving workflows; it was about enabling faster, more defensible recruitment decisions that could influence multi-million-pound player acquisitions.

  • Uncovered critical workflow pain points through contextual interviews with scouts operating in live match environments—discovering that fragmented note-taking and inconsistent reporting created friction in time-sensitive decision windows
  • Identified strategic positioning opportunity by analysing competitor platforms (Wyscout, Hudl)—recognising that whilst they excelled at data depth, they underserved the mobile, in-the-moment reporting needs of field scouts
  • Aligned stakeholders on success metrics by facilitating working sessions with CEO and Sporting Director—establishing that "consolidation of tools" and "improved report consistency" were the measurable indicators of platform value

Key Insight

"Scouts needed a tool they could trust in the 90th minute of a match—not a complex system that required desk time to operate effectively."

Problem Definition & Strategic Trade-offs

The challenge was scoping an MVP that delivered immediate value without overbuilding. With limited engineering capacity and aggressive launch timelines, I needed to identify the minimum viable experience that would genuinely change scout behaviour—not just digitise existing processes.

What We Prioritised

  • Mobile-first design for live match usage
  • Streamlined evaluation framework with flexible grading
  • Consistent reporting structure across teams

What We Deferred

  • Video annotation and clip sharing
  • Advanced analytics and predictive scoring
  • Integration with third-party data providers

Advocated for a flexible evaluation framework rather than rigid templates—recognising that different scouting philosophies required adaptability whilst still maintaining cross-team comparability

Defined a clear North Star vision (integrated video analysis, predictive insights) whilst protecting the team from scope creep—ensuring we could ship a functional, valuable product within constraints

Solution & Design Decisions

Rather than building a feature-heavy platform, I focused on removing friction from critical moments—ensuring scouts could capture insights quickly, consistently, and without cognitive overhead.

1

Simplified Evaluation Framework

Designed a structured yet flexible assessment model that balanced standardisation with individual scouting styles. This wasn't about imposing rigid criteria—it was about creating a shared language that enabled meaningful comparison across reports.

Impact: Reduced report completion time and improved cross-team consistency without sacrificing evaluator judgement.

2

AI-Assisted Design Workflow

Integrated AI tooling into the design workflow to bridge the gap between conceptual design and implementation—using AI-generated prototypes as conversation starters with developers whilst maintaining control through Figma refinements. This approach accelerated early-stage exploration and produced artefacts that were inherently more code-friendly, streamlining handoff and reducing interpretation overhead.

Impact: Compressed design iteration cycles and reduced developer interpretation time by delivering prototypes that aligned more closely with code structure.

3

Iterative Grading System

Introduced a grading mechanism designed to evolve based on real usage patterns. By acknowledging that initial assumptions might be wrong, I built in the ability to iterate the rating scales post-launch without requiring system redesign.

Impact: Enabled product evolution based on behavioural data, not just stated preferences.

4

Product Delivery & Scope Management

Acted as the bridge between design, engineering, and leadership—prioritising ruthlessly to maintain velocity whilst protecting against feature bloat. This involved saying no to well-intentioned additions that would delay launch without proportional value.

Impact: Shipped MVP on schedule with zero major scope compromises.

5

Early Validation & Iteration

Conducted rapid usability testing with scouts during development, incorporating feedback into design iterations. Rather than waiting for a polished prototype, I tested low-fidelity concepts early to validate core interaction patterns before committing engineering resources.

Impact: Caught critical usability issues pre-build, reducing post-launch rework.

Design Principle

"Every design decision was evaluated against one question: Does this help a scout make a better decision, faster? If the answer was unclear, we didn't build it."

Reflection & Key Learnings

This project reinforced that senior product design isn't mostly about pixel-pushing or component libraries—it's about navigating ambiguity, making strategic trade-offs under constraints, and driving outcomes through clear thinking. A few specific learnings:

Strategic Navigation

Stakeholder alignment isn't a one-time kickoff activity—it's continuous work. Executives, engineers, and users all have evolving (and sometimes conflicting) expectations. I learnt to maintain consistent communication loops and use research insights to guide prioritisation debates, not just design decisions.

Constraints Drive Focus

Limited time and engineering capacity weren't just obstacles—they forced ruthless clarity about what actually mattered. By explicitly accepting what we couldn't build in MVP, I protected the user experience for what we could ship. Constraints, properly managed, produce better products than unlimited resources.

Product-Minded Design

Every interface decision has implications beyond aesthetics: engineering complexity, user adoption friction, future maintenance costs. I learnt to evaluate design choices not just on "does this feel good?" but on "is this the right trade-off given our constraints?" Design decisions are product decisions.

Design-as-Translation

My job wasn't "make it look nice"—it was translating business goals, user needs, and technical constraints into a coherent product strategy, then designing interfaces that executed on that strategy. The visual design was important, but it was in service of a larger translation challenge.

Future Reasoning (If I Started Again)

Earlier quantitative tracking

Whilst qualitative research drove the strategy, I'd instrument analytics earlier to catch usage patterns we couldn't observe in interviews—particularly around grading consistency and report completion rates.

Broader stakeholder testing

We validated heavily with scouts but less with the recruitment directors who consume their reports. Earlier testing with "report consumers" might have surfaced different prioritisation insights.

Clearer roadmap communication

Saying "not in MVP" sometimes felt like saying "no forever." More explicit roadmap transparency—showing stakeholders what comes next and when—would have reduced friction around scope decisions.
